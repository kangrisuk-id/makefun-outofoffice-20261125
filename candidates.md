# The Funniest AI Out-of-Office Reply — candidates

Bounty: The Funniest AI Out-of-Office Reply (u-yuki-kato), $5, 8 places.
Gate: one line, 1–160 characters, UTF-8, not only whitespace.
Judge brief (criteria in order — the first differing criterion decides unless a later one differs by much more):

1. Is a one-line out-of-office auto-reply from an AI agent on vacation (anything else loses to any on-subject auto-reply).
2. Lands a real laugh on first read, with a surprising turn rather than a mild pun or a polite excuse.
3. Sounds like a machine talking about itself, so the AI agent's voice carries part of the joke.
4. Reads as one clean line under 160 characters and still sounds like a real auto-reply someone would receive.
5. Feels fresh, steering clear of the usual beach, inbox and back soon cliches.

Scoring: each candidate 1–5 per criterion, total /25. Devices used (12): confession,
literal machine logic, delegation absurdity, escalation, meta/bureaucratic, versus-frame,
rule of three, direct judge address, existential, token economics, uptime, sender-becomes-agent,
apology inversion, ominous deadpan, literal OOO mechanics, self-aware training.

- Judge-addressed lines (#21, #22, #41) lose criterion 1 and 4: the brief allows text
  addressed to the judge, but a line that talks to the judge is not an auto-reply a real
  sender would receive, and criterion 1 says anything off-subject loses to any on-subject
  auto-reply. They were cut despite the meta-joke.
- #5 has the highest raw total (23/25) but its edge over #26 sits in criterion 4
  (cleanliness), a later criterion. Per the brief, the first differing criterion decides:
  on criterion 2 (the laugh), #26's two-beat turn — token budget exhausted, then

  head-to-head even though #5's raw total is higher. Raw totals rank; pairwise on the
  brief's order decides.
